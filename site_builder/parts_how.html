Data
Simulate and load
What
A simulator builds 34 sources, 800 lots and 73,133 items over two years with hidden ground truth, a randomized fixed-price pilot, a photo experiment, wrong catalog matches, duplicate rows and late price revisions, and a source-mix change in June 2026.
Why
With no real data available, known truth lets every method be graded against the answer it is trying to recover. The injected defects are the ones the take-home taught: duplicates inflate joins.
Impact
Staging removes 1,097 duplicate item rows and 36 duplicate lot rows. Left in, lot cost would be overstated by 3.9%.
Warehouse
DuckDB models and tests
What
Staging models de-duplicate; marts compute every cost and recovery definition; 15 data-quality tests run on every build (13 must pass, 2 report what was cleaned).
Why
One definition of net revenue, handling, holding and allocated lot price, so every model and report agrees. Lot price is allocated to items by retail share and the test checks it ties to the lot price to the cent.
Impact
Zero failing tests. Open items carry no outcome until they close, so no model trains on unfinished results.
Model 1
Recovery rate
What
Two parts: probability it sells, times expected recovery if it sells. LightGBM on listing-time features, calibrated on a later window, with conformal 80% intervals.
Formula
E[recovery] = P(sold) x E[recovery | sold]. Target = sale price / retail value; unsold = 0.
Impact
Error 0.169 vs 0.187 (incumbent lookup) and 0.209 (category average). Total dollars within 2.9%. A sold-only model overstates by 13%.
Model 2
Sale timing
What
Discrete-time survival: one row per item per day, a boosted classifier for the daily chance of sale, with unsold items censored not dropped, then recalibrated at the window end.
Why
Fixed price has no countdown. Without a timing model you cannot say how long to wait or compare strategies fairly.
Impact
Ranks items well (Brier 0.236 vs 0.250 constant). Over-predicts after the June source change (0.638 vs 0.587): flagged by monitoring.
Decision 1
The zero problem
What
Reads the randomized pilot three ways (sold only, zeros counted, whole strategy), tests balance, and sizes the sample.
Formula
E_fixed = p(1+m)X + (1-p)(X-h). Fixed-price-first wins if p > h / (mX + h).
Impact
On average fixed-price-first is worse than auction (-0.013 per retail dollar) and takes 14 days vs 4. By category it is a clear win only for Large_appliances (+0.070); auction clearly wins for Laptops_current, Smartphones, Gaming, Beauty_health and Toys_games. Choosing per item gains +$3.94 per item over always-auction.
Decision 2
Waiting and markdowns
What
Per-category price response from the pilot, then backward induction over a price ladder to get the best price each day.
Formula
Hold one more day iff q(P - A) > (1 - q)(c + d). q = chance of sale today, P = price now, A = auction fallback, c = holding cost, d = decay.
Impact
Learned schedule 0.322 vs 0.295 for the best single price, scored against the true mechanism. 0.303 inside the tested range: confirm with a markdown test.
Decision 3
Effort
What
Randomized extra-photo experiment, uplift shrunk toward the pool, converted to dollars and compared with labor. A separate table finds the smallest band where an item earns anything.
Formula
Take extra photos iff uplift x expected sale price x (1 - fee) + avoided refunds > minutes x labor rate.
Impact
Photos pay when the expected sale price is above about $27 (roughly the $100 to $250 retail band). Below it they lose money.
Decision 4
Sources and bids
What
Source scorecard with points and tiers (empirical-Bayes shrinkage), a lot-value model, and a bid ceiling with a risk buffer, backtested on 800 lots.
Formula
Ceiling = (E[lot net value] - freight - k x sigma) / (1 + target margin).
Impact
Lot value correlates 0.979 with actual. Source score correlates 0.83 with true profitability. A strict ceiling skips 68% of lots; the sweep shows the cost.
Model 3
Match quality
What
A classifier trained on 4,388 audited matches flags likely-wrong matches and ranks a review queue by dollars at risk.
Why
Recovery is measured against the recorded retail value. A wrong match corrupts the target and the price.
Impact
Reviewing the top 5% of items captures about 75% of mismatch dollars; 20% captures 99%.
Monitoring
Is it still good?
What
Population stability index per input, monthly error against a baseline, a retrain rule, and a model card with limits.
Why
The June 2026 source change is exactly the kind of shift that breaks a model without anyone noticing.
Impact
Alerts on listing month (seasonal), source type and source id (the new regional marketplace). Retrain recommended.
Metric layer
Answer report requests
What
Governed metric definitions with seven fields each, gross vs net, week and month rules, and clarifying questions when a request is ambiguous.
Why
'Sales last week' has several honest answers. The layer asks, or states which one it used and why.
Impact
12 test questions resolve correctly: 'sales' and 'revenue' prompt a question; 'recovery' defaults to the documented gross-on-all-items measure and says so.
