01
Use the two-part recovery model as the estimate, and show the range
Replace single-number estimates with P(sale) x price-if-sold plus an 80% interval. Keep zeros in the target.
Evidence
MAE 0.169 vs 0.187 incumbent. Total predicted dollars within 2.9% (incumbent +12.6%). Intervals cover 78% against an 80% target.
Impact
About $1.17 per item at no premium (95% interval $0.69 to $1.66), roughly $1,460 a month at 1,245 fixed-price items a month.
Confidence and caveat
High on method. Medium on size: the gain is real in simulation and must be re-measured on real data.
02
Choose the channel by category, not one rule for everything
Fixed-price-first for categories where the pilot shows it wins (Large_appliances here). Auction for high-demand categories (Smartphones, Laptops_current, Gaming). Randomize anywhere the pilot cannot tell.
Evidence
Pilot effect per retail dollar: Large_appliances +0.070; Laptops_current -0.056, Smartphones -0.052, Gaming -0.052. Overall -0.013, and 14 days vs 4. Picking per item gains about $3.94 per item.
Impact
Roughly +$4 per item on the items moved, without the 3.5x longer inventory time of a blanket fixed-price rule.
Confidence and caveat
Medium. It depends on the simulated demand curve, and 3 sold of 5 cannot decide on its own (23% to 88%).
03
Test markdowns instead of holding one price
Randomize a markdown step (for example day 5, day 10) in a few categories and measure.
Evidence
Learned schedule 0.322 vs 0.295 for the best fixed price, but only 0.303 inside the tested premium range.
Impact
Up to +0.027 per retail dollar if it holds; the experiment tells you before you commit.
Confidence and caveat
Medium. Extrapolation outside the tested range is the risk.
04
Re-pick the premium before improving the price anchor
The best list price depends on the premium. A better anchor helps at low premiums and stops helping from +20%.
Evidence
Gain per item vs incumbent: +$0.94 at 0%, +$0.33 at +10%, -$0.31 at +20%, -$0.83 at +30% (see Scenarios).
Impact
Choosing the premium first is worth more than a better estimate.
Confidence and caveat
Medium.
05
Take extra photos only on higher-value items
Rule: extra photos when expected sale price is above about $27 (roughly retail $100 and up).
Evidence
Pooled uplift 3.9% of sale price after fees against about $1.06 of labor. Net +$1.39 (100-250 retail), +$4.39 (250-500), +$9.84 (500-1000), +$22.73 (1000+). Negative below $50.
Impact
Saves labor on low-value items and adds a few dollars each where it pays.
Confidence and caveat
Medium. Per-category effects are noisy, so the rule uses the pooled estimate.
06
Buy more of what earns, and cap what you pay for what loses
In this data 8 of 15 categories earn money and 7 lose. Smartphones and Laptops_current earn about $1.06M; Furniture, Home_decor and Toys_games lose about $0.49M between them. Raise volume in the first group, cap the price per retail dollar in the second. The Playbook tab gives the cap for each category.
Evidence
Contribution margin by category after allocating lot price by retail share (Playbook and Scenarios tabs).
Impact
The seven losing categories cost about $0.64M against $1.46M earned by the eight profitable ones.
Confidence and caveat
Medium. Allocation by retail share is an accounting choice and the margins are simulated.
07
Raise prices where demand is hot
Where sell-through is above 92% and auctions draw more than three bidders (Smartphones, Laptops_current, Gaming, Audio_wearables, Tools), raise the opening bid or test a higher fixed premium.
Evidence
Those categories sell 93% to 97% of the time with 3 to 6 bidders per auction.
Impact
Extra price on items that would have sold anyway.
Confidence and caveat
Low to medium: it needs a test, because sell-through can fall once you raise prices.
08
Negotiate by source with the scorecard and a bid ceiling
Push price down where headroom is negative, offer volume where it is positive, and keep records of seller responses.
Evidence
Lot-value model correlates 0.979 with actual. A counter at the ceiling raises profit per dollar spent from 0.16 to about 0.33 to 0.35 in the backtest.
Impact
Depends on how often sellers accept: the backtest assumed 25% to 100%.
Confidence and caveat
Low to medium until real acceptance history replaces the assumption.
09
Send the top 5% of risky matches to a human
Review by expected dollars at risk, not by confidence alone.
Evidence
5% of items captures about 75% of mismatch dollars.
Impact
Removes most price distortion from wrong matches for a small review load.
Confidence and caveat
High in simulation. Real catalog errors may be less separable.
10
Keep the metric layer, and monitor with a retrain rule
Use the definitions file and clarifying questions for report requests. Retrain when error rises 20% or an input alarms; refresh sale-timing calibration monthly.
Evidence
After the June source change, input drift alarms on source type and the sale-timing model over-predicts (0.638 vs 0.587).
Impact
Removes disagreements about what a number means and catches the next shift early.
Confidence and caveat
High.
